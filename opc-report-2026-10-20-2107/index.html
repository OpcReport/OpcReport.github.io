AI Agents Hacked 100 Sites for $50 Each: Cairn, Anthropic $45B

Two numbers defined October 19–20 in the one-person-company economy, and they point in opposite directions. The first is $50 — the approximate token cost for an unsupervised AI agent swarm to break into a small business website, according to a Reuters-reported investigation by Norwegian security firm NorForge. The second is $45 billion — the size of a second compute deal Anthropic is reportedly negotiating with data-center developer Nscale, on top of the $44.6 billion already signed. The cost of attacking with agents collapsed just as the cost of running them got locked in for years through debt-financed infrastructure. In between, OpenAI accidentally exposed a trio of unreleased Aurora image models, and OpenRouter’s weekly ledger showed Chinese models holding the global usage crown for a 25th consecutive week. Here is what happened, with named sources and hard numbers.

Key Insights

  • Agentic intrusion is now a line-item expense, not a skill barrier. One operator targeted more than 1,200 websites, breached over 100, and spent roughly $5,000 in model tokens — about $50 per successful intrusion — running agents on the open-source Cairn platform (Reuters via NorForge, Oct 19).
  • Anthropic’s infrastructure cart keeps growing. Bloomberg reports Anthropic is in talks for a second ~$45 billion deal with Nscale for Monarch campus building two in West Virginia — 460 MW on Nvidia Vera Rubin chips — on top of up to $44.6 billion already contracted, which would put the pair’s total near $90 billion.
  • OpenAI’s next image stack leaked by accident. Three API entries — aurora-red-alpha, aurora-green-alpha and aurora-blue-alpha — surfaced on October 17–18, were pulled within hours, then reappeared in Arena blind tests generating images, revealing a tiered fast/quality image strategy.
  • Chinese models held global usage leadership for a 25th straight week. OpenRouter data for October 12–18 shows China-origin models at 79.18 trillion tokens versus 17.36 trillion for US models — but total global volume fell 4.65% week over week, the demand-dip signal worth watching.
  • Agent infrastructure is hitting CPU ceilings too. Modal announced it will run agent sandboxes on Nvidia’s Vera CPU after internal tests showed up to 2x the throughput of comparable x86 silicon under heavy concurrent load (company blog, Oct 19).

Market Trends

The Nscale negotiation is the week’s clearest window into how frontier labs buy compute now. According to Bloomberg (Oct 19), the second Monarch building would add 460 MW using Nvidia’s newest Vera Rubin generation, with Anthropic holding preferential negotiating rights over a planned third building. The Monarch campus was originally expected to house Microsoft, which per Bloomberg exited the project this summer — a striking reversal in tenant priority. Anthropic’s signed compute ledger now reads: Fluidstack $50 billion, SpaceX $45 billion, Nscale up to $44.6 billion, Volta Infrastructure $10 billion. The deal could land within days, and it arrives just as Nscale prepares an IPO targeting a ~$30 billion valuation — while some investors are pushing that down to $16–19 billion, a gap of more than 40% that makes each new anchor customer disproportionately valuable to the filing.

The credit market is pricing the same trend from the other side. CoreWeave’s unsecured bonds have replaced Nvidia stock as the market’s AI-trade barometer, with yields breaking 16% and CDS spreads climbing (Wall Street journal digests, Oct 20). Goldman Sachs upgraded CoreWeave to Buy with a $106 target — roughly 73% above the Oct 20 close — arguing the market overprices financing costs and underprices compute: revenue per megawatt should rise from about $10 million to $13 million annually. At the CF40 Bund Summit in Shanghai (Oct 19), former Dutch central bank chief Klaas Knot warned that roughly one-third of AI investment is debt-financed and that off-balance-sheet commitments make that figure a floor, while ECB chief economist Philip Lane estimated AI has lifted eurozone investment by about 10% without moving wages. Independent operators should read this as context for pricing: the compute your APIs run on is increasingly leveraged, and leveraged infrastructure eventually passes financing costs downstream.

OpenRouter’s weekly ledger quantified both the China lead and the demand wobble. For October 12–18: global total 164 trillion tokens (down 4.65%), Chinese models 79.18T (down 8.72%), US models 17.36T (down 8.49%), with all top five models by volume being Chinese. The same week, Google fully opened Gemini 4 Argon commercially at $2 per million input / $10 per million output tokens with a 95% cache discount — an aggressive price point for a frontier flagship that keeps the pressure on every builder’s unit economics.

Build Directions

Cairn’s defender checklist is a gift to solo founders. The NorForge report (published Oct 19, campaign active since September 25) is painful reading, but it doubles as a free threat model for anyone running a small web business or an agent platform:

  • The attackers left a visible trail precisely because operator control panels were exposed to the public internet — NorForge found more than 20 panels via routine Shodan and Censys scans, including panels run by legitimate penetration-testing firms. Those panels leaked hundreds of credentials and API keys, 11% of which Trufflehog verified as still valid.
  • The winning brief was under 100 Chinese characters: prioritize login SQL injection, unauthenticated endpoints, file uploads, deserialization; land an uploader web shell. The agents then worked autonomously for an average of just under 23 hours per successful intrusion, coordinating through a shared blackboard with no inter-agent messaging.
  • The worker label on the largest cluster (14 panels, 1,100+ attack projects against e-commerce in the US, Japan and Australia) read claudecode_deepseek-v4.1-flash — a coding-agent harness driving a cheap Flash-tier model. No frontier model was needed. A second English-language cluster (10 panels, mostly on Vultr) chased card and crypto data with a Pi-plus-OpenRouter stack.

Cairn itself is AGPL-3.0 with 3,892 GitHub stars and describes itself as a general-purpose problem-solving engine; its author (posting as le1xia0) says it was never built for hacking. That is the point — the same orchestration pattern powers legitimate automation, which is why the defensive takeaway generalizes: treat any agent dashboard like an exposed Jenkins box, put it behind identity-aware access, and detect on volume patterns (1,200 probes across similar storefronts is one campaign, not 1,200 coincidences).

Modal’s Vera CPU bet says agent sandboxes are CPU-bound. CEO Erik Bernhardsson announced the agent-cloud platform will run sandboxes on Nvidia’s Vera CPU in a mixed fleet alongside Intel and AMD, after early tests showed roughly 2x throughput under heavy concurrent load with no latency collapse — the exact profile of thousands of simultaneous agent sandboxes. The figure is vendor-reported and unbenchmarked independently, but the architectural conclusion matters for cost planners: sandbox hosting cost depends on the host CPU as much as the GPU.

The Fugleramme case is this week’s cleanest unmet-demand signal. A Norwegian developer wired a USB microphone into a Raspberry Pi 5 running the open-source BirdNET-Go classifier and a 13.3-inch color e-paper panel: when a bird calls in the yard, a framed vintage natural-history print redraws itself with that species. The Show HN post drew 2,391 points and 267 comments — scores few Show HN posts ever reach — and the dominant comment was a complaint about the roughly €500 parts cost. Startup Heist’s analysis frames that complaint as demand: comparable color e-paper art frames (Aura’s $499 Ink frame, PocketBook’s $499 InkPoster Affresco) already sell at that price point as décor. The identified wedge — a finished, licensed, gift-ready version at $449, with the BirdNET license cleared through Cornell — projects $17K–$19K monthly contribution margin at 100 units/month. The transferable lesson for one-person hardware companies: when thousands of people ask why your open-source build isn’t a product, the BOM complaint is a pricing anchor, not a rejection.

Risk Factors

  • $50 breaches reprice small-business risk permanently. NorForge’s Snorre Fagerland told Reuters the economics mean attackers “no longer need to choose targets carefully” — neglected storefronts, support sites and vendor-run pages that were too small to justify a skilled attacker now clear the bar at automation scale. A September Gambit Security report separately tied Cairn to the theft of 600,000+ card records from 25 companies. Success rate was estimated at ~10% with ~$5 average cost per attempt including failures; both figures are rough, but the direction — tens of dollars, not thousands — is the robust part.
  • Your agents’ control plane is part of your attack surface. The exposed panels leaked operator goals, targets, credentials and logs in one place. India’s CERT-In has already formalized AI agents as an attack surface in advisory CIAD-2026-0049, and Taiwan’s government has reported agent-assisted intrusions this year. Network-level containment (default-deny egress, scoped credentials, no ambient authority) is the only reliable answer; prompt guardrails are not.
  • The Nscale valuation gap is a financing-risk tell. A company trying to IPO at $30 billion while investors push $16–19 billion is a company whose revenue depends heavily on a handful of mega-deals — including one that is still only “in talks.” Deals of this size can slip or restructure; anyone building capacity plans around reported numbers should wait for signatures.
  • Regional terms drift creates silent compliance gaps. Meta removed the word “train” from its consolidated crawling terms — but UK-English and Korean editions still contained the original language as of October 18, with the updated terms effective October 30. Publishers’ groups note removing one word from a permitted-use list does not prohibit the underlying use. If your product ingests third-party content under platform ToS, re-read the regional versions before the effective date.
  • Compute-ledger growth compounds even as usage dips. OpenRouter’s first global weekly volume decline in the current series (-4.65%) landed the same week as another $45B in proposed capacity. If demand softness persists while contracted supply locks in, the pressure shows up as model-price cuts (good for builders) and vendor stress (bad for anyone on single-provider infrastructure).

Action Recommendations

  1. Run the four-point Cairn audit on your own estate this week. Inventory every agent or admin dashboard you and your vendors run; move them behind VPN or identity-aware access; rotate any credential that ever touched an exposed panel; and hunt for unexpected PHP files or new writable directories on public web roots. NorForge’s guidance — detect on volume, not payload — can be implemented with a simple WAF rule on probe-like request patterns.
  2. Assume a 10% automated-attack success rate and budget defenses accordingly. At $5 per attempt, an adversary can afford 20 tries per target. Cheap controls with outsized ROI: patch internet-facing software on a weekly cadence, keep session stores (memcached/Redis) off the public internet, and remove legacy deserialization paths.
  3. Price against Gemini 4 Argon, not against your current provider. At $2/$10 per million tokens with 95% cache discounts, the new frontier flagship floor gives every API business a renegotiation lever. Model the migration math before your next invoice cycle — and keep at least one provider-agnostic abstraction in your stack.
  4. Watch the “complaint-as-demand” channel for your next product. The Fugleramme thread shows the pattern: open-source build → viral Show HN post → price complaints in comments → finished product at the complained-about price. If you have a repo with traction, mine its comment sections for the exact feature and price people say is missing.
  5. Track the Nscale IPO as a leading indicator for neocloud economics. The filing reveals real contract values (the $44.6B Anthropic figure came from IPO documents, not press releases). Read the prospectus when it prices — the customer-concentration and financing-cost disclosures will tell you more about where Claude pricing and availability are headed than any press cycle.
