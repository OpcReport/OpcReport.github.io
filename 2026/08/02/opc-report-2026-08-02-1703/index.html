Only 11% of Cited Domains Overlap — AI Search Just Split Into Three Incompatible Games

Only 11% of Cited Domains Overlap — AI Search Just Split Into Three Incompatible Games

August 2, 2026 · Issue for independent builders

Key Insights

Inference cost has collapsed. Capability is commoditized. The only genuinely scarce resource left is being found — and over the past six months that resource quietly fractured into three channels that do not talk to each other.

An analysis of 680 million citations across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity found that only 11% of cited domains are shared between ChatGPT and Perplexity. There is no such thing as “AI SEO.” There are three separate games with different rules, and optimizing for one buys you almost nothing in the others.

The willingness to cite anyone at all also varies wildly. Across 34,234 AI responses, brand citation rates came in at 0.59% for ChatGPT, 13.05% for Perplexity, and 27% for Grok — a 46x spread. If you are one person with time for exactly one distribution strategy, choosing the wrong engine doesn’t halve your results. It zeroes them.

The five numbers that should change what you do this week:

Market Trends

1. The three engines diverged mechanically — “AI SEO” is now a meaningless term

For the past year the default assumption was that ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity constituted one channel called “AI search.” The citation data breaks that assumption completely. These systems retrieve on fundamentally different architectures.

ChatGPT runs two layers: static training data underneath, Bing-powered retrieval on top. Retrieval fires mostly on commercial intent — prompts containing “review,” “comparison,” “features,” or a year trigger web search 53.5% of the time versus 18.7% for informational queries. Its single largest source is Wikipedia, at 47.9% of its top citations. Roughly 29% of what it cites was published in 2022 or earlier. This is an engine that rewards accumulated authority and is close to indifferent to freshness.

Google AI Overviews runs on Gemini over Google’s own index, but has decoupled from ranking. Semantic completeness — whether a passage answers the question standalone, without surrounding context — is the strongest predictor at r=0.87. Multi-modal pages (text plus images or video) show 156% higher selection rates than text-only. And 47% of citations come from pages ranking below position 5.

Perplexity performs a live web search on every query with no knowledge cutoff. It averages 21.87 citations per response — nearly 3x ChatGPT — which means the competition for any individual citation slot is structurally the lowest of the three. It is also extreme on recency: 82% of cited content was published within the prior 30 days, and visible year signals like “2026” in titles lift citation rates by roughly 30%.

Engine #1 source Sourcing logic Realistic for a solo builder?
ChatGPT Wikipedia (47.9% of top cites) Accumulated encyclopedic authority No — years of third-party authority required
Google AI Overviews Reddit Semantic completeness + multimodal Maybe — needs images/video production
Perplexity Reddit (46.7% of top cites) Live retrieval, recency, structure Yes — the only one you can win alone

The read for a one-person operation: don’t fight on three fronts. Perplexity is the only engine where solo output can actually move the needle — many citation slots, low per-slot competition, and it rewards freshness and structure rather than domain age and backlink history. ChatGPT is the hardest target and should be last.

2. The communities AI trusts most are actively hardening against AI content

This is the most counterintuitive signal this week, and the most useful.

Reddit accounts for 46.7% of Perplexity’s top citation sources and is the leading source for Google AI Overviews. Real humans answering real questions in topic communities is, right now, the corpus AI engines trust most.

At exactly the same time, Hacker News formalized a guideline prohibiting generated or AI-edited comments — “HN is for conversation between humans.” The discussion pulled 4,229 points and 1,668 comments, among the highest-engagement meta threads the site has seen.

These two facts sit in direct tension, and the tension is the opportunity. The more AI engines depend on community content, the harder those communities work to keep AI content out. The result is that authentic human presence in high-trust communities is becoming a distribution asset that cannot be scripted or scaled — and is therefore appreciating in value.

You cannot automate your way into it. That is precisely why it is worth something. A solo builder willing to spend three focused hours a week genuinely answering questions on Reddit, HN, or a niche forum is accumulating citation surface that competitors cannot buy.

The corollary is a hard boundary: running agents to farm community posts is now a terminal move. What gets banned is your account and your domain, and what you lose is access to the 46.7% source pool. Don’t.

3. Tooling vendors are removing cost visibility — which proves observability has value

Cursor removed dollar-cost figures from its usage page and CSV export this week. The HN thread hit 324 points and 147 comments. A Cursor employee responded in-thread that the CSV break was accidental — collateral damage from cleaning up a feature flag, since fixed — and that the dollar graph was pulled deliberately because included-plan usage displayed in dollars was being misread as actual billing.

User reaction was uniform anyway, and the specific complaint is worth noting: the circular cost indicator next to the context-usage meter also disappeared, which makes it far easier to leave an expensive model running all day without noticing.

Buried in the same thread was a more actionable finding. A developer benchmarked ten agentic tasks with the same model in the same environment across different harnesses and got wildly different token consumption — roughly 173K tokens on the leanest harness versus 427K on another. Same model, same task, more than 2x the bill. Others noted that Claude Code injects a large memory-system prompt that can double cost on many-turn, low-context jobs.

The build signal: when upstream platforms trend toward opaque billing, third-party cost attribution acquires an independent reason to exist. This isn’t a grand narrative — it’s a well-defined small-SaaS gap. One commenter in that thread was already building it and said he’d now have to add Cursor support. Demand is validated in public.

Build Directions

1. Perplexity-first content engine — the best solo starting point

What to build: Pick a vertical you actually understand. Ship 3–5 structured long-form pieces per week answering questions your customers really ask. Year and question phrasing in titles and H2s. Direct answer in the first 30% of the page.

Stack: Batch-tier model for drafts — DeepSeek V4 Flash at ~$0.09/$0.18 per 1M tokens, or GLM 5.2 at $0.33/$1.05 — with human fact-checking and opinion injection on top. Astro or Hexo static site with FAQPage and Article schema. Cloudflare Pages for hosting.

Cost and timeline: $20–60/month in inference, near-zero hosting. Expect 6–8 weeks from zero to first citations.

Why now: 21.87 citation slots per response combined with 82% of cited content being under 30 days old is the only door still open to a brand-new domain.

2. Cross-engine AI visibility monitoring SaaS

What to build: User enters their brand and 20–50 high-intent prompts. You run them daily across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Grok, logging who gets mentioned, which sources got cited, and where competitors appear. Ship it as a daily digest plus change alerts.

Stack: Scheduled jobs on cron or Cloudflare Workers hitting each provider’s API. Batch-tier model for response parsing and entity extraction. Supabase for historical diffs. Resend for digest delivery.

Cost and timeline: $50–200/month all-in. MVP in 2–3 weeks.

Why now: A 46x spread in cross-platform citation rates means every brand needs per-platform data — intuition is guaranteed to misallocate budget. Players like Leapd exist, but they’re all chasing the general market. Verticals (law firms, dental, B2B manufacturing) are effectively uncovered.

3. Third-party authority placement as a service

What to build: Systematically place small SaaS companies on G2, Capterra, industry directories, comparison pages, and editorial backlinks. Unglamorous work, unusually high leverage.

The leverage: Domains with active G2 or Capterra profiles show 3x higher ChatGPT citation probability. Distributing content across multiple publications rather than your own site alone can increase AI citations by up to 325%.

Stack: Barely technical. Airtable for pipeline, Claude or GPT for outreach drafting, human send and follow-up. Sell it as a service for cash first, productize after validation.

4. Citation-readiness audits — fastest path to revenue

What to build: Restructure existing content sites for citation. Move answers into the first 30%, add TL;DR blocks, implement FAQPage schema, split paragraphs so each stands alone.

The evidence: 44.2% of LLM citations come from the first 30% of a page. Pages with FAQ schema and inline citations carry roughly 40% higher citation weighting in ChatGPT. Structured data markup correlates with a 73% selection-rate improvement.

Why it sells: No content rewrite required — you’re changing structure. Fast delivery, measurable outcome, low decision friction for the buyer. Works well as a front-end lead magnet for direction #2.

5. Agent cost attribution and alerting

What to build: Convert token flow from coding agents into real-time dollars, attributed by project, branch, and task, with threshold alerts and same-task comparison across harnesses.

Stack: Local CLI or proxy layer intercepting API calls, SQLite or Postgres for the ledger, terminal dashboard or web panel.

Why now: Upstream is actively blurring the bill, and measured token consumption for identical tasks varies by multiples across harnesses. The savings are real money, so willingness to pay is unambiguous.

Risk Factors

  • No strategy is portable. With 11% domain overlap, work done for one engine mostly evaporates in another. Multi-platform means multiplied cost, not reused effort.
  • Freshness is a recurring expense, not an asset. With 82% of Perplexity citations under 30 days old, visibility decays the moment you stop publishing. Model it as a subscription cost, not a moat.
  • Automating community presence is self-destructive. HN has codified and is enforcing its ban on generated comments; Reddit governance is tightening similarly. You’d be getting banned from precisely the sources AI engines trust most.
  • The GEO/AEO tooling space is crowding fast. General-purpose visibility tools already have multiple funded entrants. Solo entry only survives by going deep on a vertical where you bring the prompt library and channel relationships.
  • Discount the conversion figures. The 11x conversion lift and 325% citation improvement come largely from vendors selling these services. The direction is credible; validate the multiples yourself before setting budget against them.

Action Recommendations

If you’re starting out or working alone:

  • ✅ Zero-cost fix you can finish this week: move core answers into the first 30% of every page, add TL;DR blocks and FAQPage schema
  • ✅ Pick Perplexity and only Perplexity — put year signals and explicit question phrasing in titles and H2s
  • ✅ Establish a 3-posts-per-week cadence using batch-tier drafting plus your own verification. Consistency beats per-post polish
  • ✅ Reserve 3 hours weekly for genuine human participation in Reddit, HN, or a niche forum — no links, no AI drafting
  • ✅ Stand up a cost dashboard converting agent tokens to dollars before the invoice teaches you the lesson

If you have a product or a small team:

  • ✅ Spend week one on baseline measurement: run 30–50 high-intent prompts across all four engines and find out where you actually exist
  • ✅ Allocate budget proportional to citation rate, not evenly — 0.59% and 13.05% do not deserve identical spend
  • ✅ Build out third-party authority placements (G2, Capterra, directories); this is an unavoidable prerequisite on the ChatGPT path
  • ✅ Distribute content off your own domain — multi-channel distribution moves citations far more than one additional post
  • ✅ Retire organic rank as your AI visibility metric; the correlation fell from 76% to 38% or lower. Change the dashboard

What It Means

Two quarters ago the binding constraint was capability. One quarter ago it was cost. Both got solved, and neither is where advantage lives now.

What’s left is distribution — and distribution just stopped being a single thing you can be good at. It’s three engines with 11% overlap, 46x different appetites for citing anyone, and a top source pool (community content) that is actively fortifying itself against the automation you’d normally reach for.

The uncomfortable conclusion is that the highest-leverage move available to a solo builder in August 2026 isn’t technical. It’s picking one engine, publishing on a schedule you can actually sustain, and showing up as a real person in the communities the models read. None of that scales. That’s the point — anything that scaled would already have been competed away by people with more capital than you.

Pick Perplexity. Fix your page structure this week. Show up in the forums. Watch your token bill.
