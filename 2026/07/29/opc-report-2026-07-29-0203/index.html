Product Hunt Graveyard Data: A Quarter of Top Launches Are Dead — and Open Source Survives Best

A newly released “graveyard” dataset of 2,291 top Product Hunt launches just handed indie builders the most useful survival data of the year: AI products die at exactly the same rate as non-AI products, while open-source products barely die at all. Meanwhile, capital keeps piling into the model layer — Nvidia’s ~$5B bet on SSI being the loudest signal — which means the real opportunity for solo builders keeps sliding down-stack toward workflow-embedded tools. Here’s what to build and what to build it with.

Key Insights

  • The “AI” label is now worthless as a moat. The Product Hunt Graveyard dataset (2,291 top launches, 2023–2025, CC BY 4.0) probed every product’s live website and found a 24.4% hard-death rate overall — with AI products dying at 24.5% vs 24.4% for non-AI. Statistically identical. Slapping “AI-powered” on a landing page buys you nothing.
  • Open source is the cheapest survival insurance available. Open-source products in the same dataset died at just 8.9% — by far the lowest of any category. Distribution via GitHub, community maintenance, and lower trust friction compound into real staying power.
  • The model layer is in a capital frenzy; the app layer is where solo economics work. Nvidia is reportedly investing ~$5B in Ilya Sutskever’s SSI (still pre-product) with access to the next-gen Vera Rubin platform, while Naveen Rao’s Unconventional AI raised a $475M seed at a $4.5B valuation two months after founding. “Circular financing” chatter is getting louder. Don’t compete there — sell shovels below it.
  • Open-weight models are your cost weapon. BenchLM’s July leaderboard (295 models tracked) crowns MiniMax M3 as the top open-weight model. The gap with closed frontier models keeps narrowing — route high-frequency calls to cheap open-weight or small models, reserve frontier models for low-frequency, high-value steps.

Market Trends

1. Survival has decoupled from “AI” and re-coupled to distribution

The Graveyard dataset classified each launch as alive, dead, parked, or absorbed, with per-row evidence (DNS errors, HTTP status, redirect chains). Three takeaways for builders:

  • Users and investors now treat AI as a default ingredient, not a differentiator. Conversion premiums from AI positioning have evaporated.
  • The 8.9% open-source death rate reflects structural advantages: GitHub as a free distribution channel, community forks as a maintenance backstop, and auditability as a trust accelerant.
  • “Absorbed” is a legitimate outcome. Small tools that fill a gap in a larger platform get acquired rather than abandoned — a viable exit path worth designing for.

2. Agent deployment is getting “webified” — the ease-of-launch war has started

Product Hunt’s monthly chart featured a tool letting developers deploy AI agents in minutes, like deploying a web page (EdgeOne Makers, top-five finish with the second-highest raw upvotes on the board). The same week, Dank — an open-source, Docker-native agent orchestration framework — pushed a “one npm install, first agent running in under 5 minutes” pitch.

  • Agent infrastructure is moving from the framework wars (the LangChain vs LlamaIndex era) to a deployment-experience war.
  • The enterprise end is going the opposite direction: deepset/Haystack is selling “sovereign AI” — bring your own infrastructure, models, and data boundaries — to governments and regulated industries.
  • The gap between the two ends is the solo builder’s lane: vertical, hosted agents that combine dead-simple deployment with domain-specific data and workflows.

3. Model-layer capital heat vs app-layer cost deflation

Nvidia’s ~$5B SSI deal and Unconventional AI’s record-scale seed round both point to the same pattern: chip vendors taking stakes in model companies that then buy compute — the “circular financing” loop critics are flagging. For app builders the actionable line is different: open-weight quality (MiniMax M3 topping the open-weight board) means your unit economics no longer depend on frontier-model pricing. Assume closed-model API pricing is volatile; architect for model portability from day one.

Build Directions

The two questions every report should answer: what should I build, and with which tools? Here are four concrete directions.

Direction 1: Open-core + paid cloud (the model the data endorses)

  • What: Pick a developer pain you know well — log analysis, cron monitoring, form backends, webhook debugging. Open-source the core; sell a hosted/team version. The 8.9% death rate is your proof-of-thesis.
  • Stack: Claude Code (terminal agent mode to autonomously build and test the library) + GitHub (primary distribution) + Supabase or Fly.io (hosted backend) + Polar or GitHub Sponsors (monetization).
  • Cost/timeline: $20–50/mo, MVP in 2–4 weeks.

Direction 2: One-click vertical agents

  • What: Skip general agent platforms (big-vendor territory). Ship an out-of-the-box agent for one industry — real-estate lead follow-up, clinic appointment rescheduling, e-commerce returns support. Sell “live in 5 minutes, industry playbook included.”
  • Stack: Dify or Haystack (orchestration) + Claude Haiku / MiniMax M3 (cheap models for high-frequency conversation) + Docker on Fly.io (take the containerized deployment cue from Dank) + Stripe subscriptions.
  • Cost/timeline: from $50–150/mo; go deep on one vertical before adding a second.

Direction 3: Sell shovels to solopreneurs — monitoring and distribution tools

  • What: The indie products showing traction this week cluster in one category: community keyword monitoring (Syften), programmatic SEO (MetaBoost), curated success databases (OneManDB — a one-time-payment database of 1,000+ founders making $10K+/mo). The indie ecosystem is growing; tools serving it grow with it.
  • Stack: Reddit/HN APIs + GPT-4o-mini or Claude Haiku for classification and scoring (<$0.001 per item) + Resend for email digests + Lemon Squeezy for one-time or low-tier pricing.
  • Cost/timeline: shippable and charging within 1–3 weeks.

Direction 4: Data as the product, one-time pricing

  • What: The Graveyard dataset and OneManDB validate the same play: a hand-cleaned, maintained niche dataset is a sellable product on its own. Pick a high-value audience (a buyer type, a tool category, a failure-case archive) and package it as a buy-once database.
  • Stack: Claude Code for scraping/cleaning scripts + Airtable or SQLite + Gumroad for one-time sales. Cold-start trick: release ~10% of the data free under CC BY (exactly the Graveyard playbook) to earn distribution.

Risk Factors

  • Clone speed: monitoring/directory/SEO tools are trivially copyable — without a proprietary data source or community, you’re in a price war.
  • Circular-financing aftershocks: if chip-vendor-funded model companies pull back, API pricing and free tiers could swing hard. Don’t build unit economics on “inference always gets cheaper.”
  • Platform dependency: products built on Reddit/HN/Twitter APIs face repricing or lockout at any time. Keep at least two data sources.
  • Energy-constrained compute: AI’s power draw is now a headline conference topic (TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 has a dedicated energy track with fusion CEOs on stage). Long-term inference costs may not fall monotonically.

Action Recommendations

If you’re just starting out:

  • ✅ Open-source your project’s core on GitHub this week — the data says it’s the cheapest survival insurance you can buy.
  • ✅ Ship a Direction-3 monitoring MVP in 2 weeks with Claude Code + Supabase; get 10 paying users before expanding scope.
  • ✅ Benchmark MiniMax M3 / Claude Haiku for every high-frequency call path; reserve frontier models for the few steps that need them.

If you already have a product or a small team:

  • ✅ Audit workflow embedding: would users miss your product after a week away? If not, add daily touchpoints (email digests, Slack alerts) before adding features.
  • ✅ Evaluate repackaging existing capability as a one-click vertical agent — deployment experience is the new pricing power.
  • ✅ Treat “absorbed” as a plannable exit: identify exactly which platform’s gap your product fills, and build the integration story deliberately.

Quick Data Reference

  • Product Hunt top-launch hard-death rate: 24.4% (2023–2025, n=2,291)
  • AI vs non-AI death rate: 24.5% vs 24.4% — no difference
  • Open-source death rate: 8.9% — lowest of all categories
  • Nvidia → SSI: ~$5B plus Vera Rubin platform access
  • Unconventional AI: $475M seed, $4.5B post-money, 2 months after founding
  • Lightweight-model classification cost: <$0.001/item — ample margin for monitoring products

What it means: the market just told you, with data, that AI branding is free and therefore worthless — while open distribution and workflow lock-in are scarce and therefore valuable. Build small, open the core, deploy in minutes, charge for the hosted convenience, and let the model layer fight its billion-dollar war above your head.
